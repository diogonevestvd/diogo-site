AI-assisted Chinese to English translation

What changes when the text moves from news to literature?

This academic study compared two Chinese-to-English translation tasks supported by AI and human post-editing: a contemporary CCTV news article and an excerpt from Tao Yuanming’s classical text The Peach Blossom Spring. The aim was not to claim professional Chinese competence, but to examine where technology helps, where it becomes less sufficient, and why target-language judgement remains essential.

Scope and integrity

The limitation is part of the study, not something to hide.

I do not have sufficient autonomous command of Chinese to present this as professional Chinese translation. Chinese functioned as a source language mediated by AI, research and external validation, while English was the target language I could evaluate and revise.

The news case could be checked more objectively through structure, data, external research and a native Chinese reader. The literary case is explicitly treated as an analytical translation proposal that would require specialist review for professional use.

01 / Research question

How does AI usefulness change when the genre changes?

The study asked how translation difficulty changes when moving from a modern Chinese news article to a classical literary text while keeping the Chinese-to-English direction and using digital support. The hypothesis was that AI would be more useful for orientation and post-editing in informational text, but less sufficient when interpretation, voice, ambiguity and rhythm become central.

02 / Method

Same direction. Different communicative demands.

The methodology combined case-study analysis, AI-assisted translation, human post-editing and comparative evaluation. The criteria were genre, communicative function, structural predictability, lexical and cultural difficulty, need for reformulation, dependence on the target language and risk of invisible error.

1

News case

CCTV article on agricultural technology, water saving, mechanization and grain production. The final English version preserved titles, paragraphs, images and the reading structure of the original website.

2

Literary case

Excerpt from 桃花源记, attributed to Tao Yuanming. The text introduced concision, ambiguity, cultural imagery and rhythm that could not be judged by semantic adequacy alone.

3

AI role

Used as support for initial comprehension and draft generation, not as final authority. Outputs were revised for meaning, register, naturalness, units, presentation and literary effect.

4

Evaluation

The final analysis compared what could be checked objectively with what required interpretive judgement, while documenting limitations and validation needs.

03 / News case

In informational text, AI was useful as an accelerator, not an editor-in-chief.

The CCTV article had a predictable journalistic structure and many explicit information units. AI helped with first-pass comprehension, but the initial output still required reformulation. The main issues were literalness, register, Chinese units and the need for natural English news prose.

亩 / 斤

Chinese units that are not transparent to most English readers. The study used conversion or explanation where that improved readability.

覆膜穴播滴灌

A condensed technical sequence rendered as “plastic film mulching, precision hole sowing and drip irrigation” to make the agricultural process intelligible.

News register

Sentence structure and repetition were revised so the result read as English journalism rather than a syntactic copy of Chinese.

Digital format

The translation preserved article hierarchy and allowed readers to switch between Chinese and English on the project website.

04 / Literary case

Literary translation made the limits of automatic plausibility much clearer.

The excerpt from The Peach Blossom Spring required decisions about concision, ambiguity, rhythm, imagery and atmosphere. AI could suggest approximate meaning, but the quality question changed from “is the information correct?” to “what reading experience does this create in English?”

豁然开朗

Rendered as “it suddenly opened out before him”, prioritizing the experience of sudden spatial and visual revelation.

黄发垂髫

Rendered as “the old and the young”. The study explicitly notes that this improves clarity but loses part of the original metonymic imagery.

Rhythm

The movement from narrow passage to broad landscape had to be felt in the English prose, not merely reported.

Status of result

The literary translation is presented as an analytical proposal, not a professional final translation.

06 / Professional responsibility

Target-language competence is the line the study refuses to blur.

The paper argues that tools may help a translator partially understand a source language they do not master, but they cannot make professional output defensible in a target language the translator cannot evaluate. Chinese-to-English was methodologically explorable because the English result could be controlled. English-to-Chinese under the same personal conditions would not be professionally acceptable.

Validation and limits

For the news article, structure, data, units and main information could be checked through external research and comparison, and a native Chinese speaker provided an external reading. For the literary case, the paper argues that proper professional validation would require both a reader competent in classical Chinese and an English literary reviewer.

07 / Conclusion

The useful question is not whether AI replaces the translator. It is what the translator remains responsible for.

The study concludes that AI can accelerate comprehension and drafting, especially in informational genres, but does not guarantee purpose, ethics, register or literary value. The translator still has to accept, alter or reject machine output, explain the process, recognize limitations and take responsibility for the target text.
