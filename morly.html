Main case study · Active product

Morly

Love better, not louder.

A private app for couples. It helps two people preserve memories, open private conversations, revisit their relationship history and share selected moments only when they choose to.

Type
Private relationship app
Role
Product concept, UX strategy, brand direction, information architecture
Status
In development

01 / Problem

People communicate constantly, yet often avoid the conversations that matter most.

Messaging apps already exist. Morly explores the space between casual conversation and formal therapy: appreciation, discomfort, recurring issues, intimacy, memories and small emotional truths that are often scattered, avoided or forgotten.

Private first. Social only when chosen.

02 / Architecture

A five-part product system.

Home

Private daily relationship dashboard with a controlled social option.

Moments

Relationship archive: timeline, albums, monthly recaps and annual rewind.

Add

Camera-first creation flow for moments, memories, notes, conversations, wishes and posts.

Talks

Private conversations and topics that need attention.

Us

Couple profile, relationship hub, privacy and settings.

03 / Identity model

Individual login. Shared couple profile.

Users need individual accounts for security and data ownership, while the social object is the shared couple profile. That distinction keeps Morly from becoming just another individual social network.

Individual account Shared couple profile Private relationship space Controlled social layer

04 / Privacy logic

The product only works if sensitive content cannot leak into the public space.

Private by design

  • Talks
  • Sensitive notes
  • Private memories
  • Relationship history

Shareable by choice

  • Selected moments
  • Approved posts
  • Milestones
  • Safe recaps

05 / MVP discipline

V1 should prove the private couple experience before overbuilding the public network.

06 / Language inside products

A localized string is part of an interface, not an isolated sentence.

Building Morly requires decisions about labels, onboarding promises, privacy wording, recurring terminology and emotional tone inside real product flows. That perspective is directly relevant to localization QA: Portuguese has to be accurate and natural, but it also has to fit the action, hierarchy and trust requirements of the interface.

UI labelsOnboarding languagePrivacy wordingTerminology consistencyTone across flows

What this demonstrates

Morly adds product context to the language portfolio: interface architecture, privacy, UX decisions and the constraints localized copy has to work within.
