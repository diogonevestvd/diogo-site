02 / MT and post-editing

Grammatically correct is not the same as linguistically ready.

One task started from a machine translation of an economic text. The exercise required comparing the source, automatic output and human revision, then identifying segments that required intervention. The analysis exposed common MT limitations: literalness, unsuitable register, terminology inconsistency and formulations that appear acceptable in isolation but fail in the wider text.

The main lesson was to separate surface correctness from actual quality: a sentence can be grammatical and still sound artificial, unprofessional or inappropriate for its context.

03 / Terminology + CAT

Consistency should come from a system, not memory.

Terminology work included an EN-PT bilingual termbase and later a larger memoQ project based on a guide to doing business in the UK. That project required managing segments, terminology, consistency, translation and a translation note. It was ultimately completed individually, requiring autonomy across the full workflow.

06 / Method development

The most useful part of the portfolio was identifying how to make the workflow more rigorous.

The final reflection did not treat the exercises as perfect outputs. It identified weaknesses and converted them into concrete changes to the working method.

01

Monolingual review

Read the final text only in Portuguese to catch calques and unnatural wording.

02

Terminology

Record source, context, variant and reliability level in terminology resources.

03

Post-editing

Classify edits by error type to make interventions easier to justify.

04

Technical QA

Reserve specific time for formatting, export checks and final segment verification.
